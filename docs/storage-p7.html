marco storage, phase 7: the cutover gates

Why each gate phase 6 failed failed, and what changed. The gates and their numbers are in docs/storage-results.html; this page is the diagnosis. Written by internal/p7_report.py from internal/raw-p7.

1. The read path at Fits: a metric and whole-block decodes

bytes-used accounting · decoding sets and lists · finding lists and pages · DID directory · everything else (RPC, HTTP, allocation, the kernel)

Server CPU per request, Fits, skewed v2, one server under a closed loop of 512 in flight, 20 s of perf at 499 Hz: a symbol's share of the samples times 16 busy threads over the loop's rate. Rows move between categories with inlining from build to build; the rates beside the bars are the measurement.

Phase 6 put the gap in FORMAT 6's layout because FORMAT 6 over mmap was as slow as the engine. Both ran the same read path, and the gap was two things on it. The path measured the bytes every decode used on every pass, scanning varints and Elias-Fano high bits a second time to find each block's end: 7.6 µs of a 42.4 µs request. Counting one pass in 32 per thread, scaled back up, keeps the bytes read ÷ bytes used estimate and gave +6.6%. Then membership (Has, Relationships' in-set checks, the probes of Intersect and known followers, a big list's by-subject lookups) decoded a whole block of 128 ids to binary-search it. It now selects the id's bucket in the block's high bits and reads only that bucket's low bits (inset::block_find); a block more than 8 probes land in is still decoded once. That gave +7.1%, and FORMAT 6 over mmap went from 88.7% of FORMAT 5 to 101.1%; the engine is at 98.2%. Decodes also read both bit fields as words from the page in place rather than from slices cut to each field's length, which sent every block's last words through a byte copy.

2. Catch-up at Fits: a third pass over every write's reads

E5 at Fits: the archive day (2.75M ops) as fast as the server takes it, under the skewed v2 load at 50%. The writer is one thread, and it was busy the whole catch-up: perf over 12 s of it counted 5,367 of its samples at 499 Hz (6,000 is all of them), 62% of them reads (finding and decoding lists, the directory) against 57% under mmap. A write's reads run once on each of the two served copies, and over the engine the writer first read every batch's reads ahead of them (Graph::plan), so that a batch that misses waits on disk once per dependency level. At full residency nothing misses, and the plan was a third pass, with every page it touched pinned and unpinned. It now plans only while writes miss: a plan whose first pass finds everything turns planning off, and a write's read that misses turns it back on for the next batch.

That took the engine from 132,246 to 162,422 ops/s, 81% of mmap prefault's 199,700 on the same tree (the gate is 95%). What's left isn't misses: the client's scrapes count none in the catch-up. Nor is it the policy's frame state: a write's reads now skip it (Hold::write: no use counted, hits not attributed to a queue), and the rate didn't move. A follow-up profiles the writer on this tree; at 2.75M ops a day the catch-up takes 17 s against 14.

2b. The week: compaction by what a rewrite gives back

Free memory in the budget before each day's checkpoint, against what that checkpoint took (P_new + R); red where it didn't fit. Phase 6 compacted nothing on days 1-4: compaction counted only pages no index names, and refused when those couldn't cover the shortfall. Old versions of lists mostly sit in pages that still hold live lists, so a rewrite gives back far more than the dead pages. Compaction now ranks shards by that (all the shard's paged bytes less what a rewrite would hold: the base's, scaled by its records now over the base's) and takes them until the next checkpoint fits and what's left to win back is under 1 GiB, decision 67's close to every shard a day. A checkpoint gives up the compaction rather than waiting out its shard, which held days 5 and 6 to 100 and 131 s. A simulated day is 150 s, so the next one starts once that compaction is done or 10 minutes have passed, with background I/O unpaced. A shard's compaction took 14.0 s unpaced (292 shards over the week) against 34.2 s paced at 200 MB/s in phase 6 (28 shards), so a day of them paced is about 37 minutes of a real day. A first run of this week found a third thing: the compaction thread held the snapshot it started from, and with it every compacted shard's old files and their cached pages. Over its first 33 shards free stayed between 0.2 and 0.9 GiB, and the new bases' pages pushed 3.4 GiB of live pages out of the main queue. It now takes the served copy's snapshot afresh for each shard (raw-p7/2/week-held-snapshot, stopped on day 1). Before day 1's checkpoint, with nothing compacted yet, free was 2.24 GiB and that checkpoint took 2.24: at Fits the 44 GiB budget leaves a checkpoint about the room it takes, where §11 planned 3.4 GB, so whatever a day's compaction hasn't won back by the next checkpoint fails it. This tree's week held it on 2 of 7 days.

3. Tight: the deltas beside the budget

phase 6 (OOM-killed in the checkpoint) · this tree

Tight is an 8 GiB cgroup with --memory 6GiB. The day caught up in 51 s with the cgroup at 7.9 GiB, and 37 s into the checkpoint the kernel killed the server at 8.36 GB: --memory covered the store, and beside it the deltas of a day in both served copies took about 1.4 GiB, and the checkpoint's replayed copy half as much again. Nothing watched them. Now the server does, every 200 ms: its RSS less the store's books (committed frames, pinned sections, tables) is what it holds beside the store, and whatever of that is past what the limit leaves (the limit, less --memory, less 512 MiB) it borrows from the budget in 64 MiB pieces, which gives back cache. The limit is the cgroup's memory.max unless --memory-limit says otherwise. Counting the deltas from what they actually take, instead of estimating their maps, also counts the checkpoint's copy, the request heap and the allocator's slack, which are exactly what took the last gigabyte.

4. Half: what sets p99

Half, skewed v2, 4 server threads, at 50% and 90% of F6 mmap random's sustained rate (90,897/s). The gate asks the engine's p99 to be at most half of random's at the same load: at 90% it's 2.42 ms against 4.61 ms (0.52×), at 50% 0.42 ms against 0.55 ms (0.76×; phase 6's engine 0.58 ms), and p999 at 90% is 4.73 ms. Every shape's p99 sits in one band, from one Has to a page of follows: the tail is time a request waits for one of 4 threads, not time spent reading, and random advice keeps the skewed working set as resident as the engine does. The engine's cheaper requests (§1) shortened that wait; halving it at half load would take halving the queue.
