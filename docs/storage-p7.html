1. The read path at Fits: a metric and whole-block decodes

bytes-used accounting · decoding sets and lists · finding lists and pages · DID directory · everything else (RPC, HTTP, allocation, the kernel)

Server CPU per request, Fits, skewed v2, one server under a closed loop of 512 in flight, 20 s of perf at 499 Hz: a symbol's share of the samples times 16 busy threads over the loop's rate. Rows move between categories with inlining from build to build; the rates beside the bars are the measurement.

Phase 6 put the gap in FORMAT 6's layout because FORMAT 6 over mmap was as slow as the engine. Both ran the same read path, and the gap was two things on it. The path measured the bytes every decode used on every pass, scanning varints and Elias-Fano high bits a second time to find each block's end: 7.6 µs of a 42.4 µs request. Counting one pass in 32 per thread, scaled back up, keeps the bytes read ÷ bytes used estimate and gave +6.6%. Then membership (Has, Relationships' in-set checks, the probes of Intersect and known followers, a big list's by-subject lookups) decoded a whole block of 128 ids to binary-search it. It now selects the id's bucket in the block's high bits and reads only that bucket's low bits (inset::block_find); a block more than 8 probes land in is still decoded once. That gave +7.1%, and FORMAT 6 over mmap went from 88.7% of FORMAT 5 to 101.1%; the engine is at 98.2%. Decodes also read both bit fields as words from the page in place rather than from slices cut to each field's length, which sent every block's last words through a byte copy.
