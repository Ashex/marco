has < 300 ns is not met, and P1 didn't move it: 1.4 µs at p50 under live ingest, as at P0. The API's has takes two DIDs and returns the edge's URI, so it pays two directory lookups (350 ns each), the rkey's lookup on a hit, and formatting the URI. On ids, a miss is 121 ns and a hit 661 ns. Meeting it as written takes a boolean membership call on ids, which an appview could use once it holds marco's ids for the viewer and the authors it hydrates; or the target moves to what has actually returns. That's a scope call for P2, not a durability problem.

The Jetstream tail clause holds for everything marco adds: its share of the lag never passed 205 ms. End to end it held at p99.9 (536 ms) but not at the max: one upstream spike put 106 ops 3783 ms behind, and the null sinks on two us-east instances saw the same spike at the same minute. marco can't make a source faster, so the clause should read as marco's added lag, with the source's measured beside it.

Durability as built

No write-ahead log. A kill loses at most the last 5 s of applied writes, and the sources replay them from the newest segment's cursors. Tombstones and statuses live in the shards, so a replay lands the same way.

Replaying more than needed is exact because every op carries its source's event time. A delete leaves a tombstone with that time, an add seen earlier than the tombstone stays deleted, and an add seen later re-creates. Checkpoints drop tombstones older than 7 days, past either source's replay window (Jetstream 36 h, the relay 72 h).

Restart to ready with more than a day of segments (a day of follows and blocks is ~2.7M ops): open, replay the segments, then the source catches up from the newest segment's cursor. Ready means every source is under 1 s behind.

Each run copies the whole-network snapshot twice: marco's, and the expected state, which the harness builds by applying the whole capture once in-process. A fake source replays the capture at a fixed rate from the first connection, so the kills land mid-ingest, and marco restarts on its own copy and resumes from its newest segment's cursor. At the end every URI and DID the capture touched is compared through Admin.ResolveUri and Admin.GetActor: rel, subject, the pair's record count, both sides of the in set, and per relation the out, in and mutual counts and the actor's status.

Kills during a checkpoint come two ways. Forced: Admin.Checkpoint, then a kill within 300 ms, which lands in the checkpoint's first phase (its own replay of the segments) before any shard file is written. Automatic: with --checkpoint-ops 30000 marco checkpoints on its own every ~15 min of the capture, and random kills of 1 to 90 s land anywhere in one. In that run 2 checkpoints were killed 15 s, 10 s into writing shard files, 2 committed in runs that were killed later (writing took 22 s, 22 s), and 2 more committed in the final run. 30k ops touch all 64 shards, so each is a full rewrite.

The first Jetstream run stopped after 19 kills without comparing: it combined forced and automatic checkpoints, and the harness's Admin.Checkpoint call found one already running and gave up. marco refused the second checkpoint as designed; the harness should take that as the moment it wanted and kill anyway (task 949). So the gate runs forced and automatic checkpoints separately.

The firehose run has no restart-to-ready figure: the fake relay replays frames with their recorded times, so a restarted marco is minutes behind by its clock and never reports ready. Caught up there means its cursor passed the pre-kill high-water mark.

Ingest doesn't move reads. Under live Jetstream (~31 ops/s while reads ran), has and relationships match CR 1082's idle-box numbers to within a few percent, and so they do with the capture replayed at 10 times its rate (~348 ops/s). relationships meets its budget at p50, as in CR 1082; its p99 is about twice that. The writer's ceiling holds with a source attached, since a live source is a few batches a second. Delete by URI stays under 5 µs at p99 after 3M ops of deltas, the most a checkpoint lets build up; it's timed on the engine, which is what the writer runs per op.

Restart to ready with more than a day of segments (4,458,506 ops in 16 segments; a day of follows and blocks is ~2.7M) took 10.9 to 11.7 s: about 10 s of replay, then under a second for the source to catch up from the newest segment's cursor. With the page cache dropped first, CR 1082 measured the replay alone at 13.3 s. In the kill tests, where checkpoints keep the segments short, restart to ready is under a second.

marco and the null sink on the same instance see the same tail: p50 101 ms against 99 ms, p99 163 ms against 163 ms. Matched op by op, marco's own share is 2.7 ms at p50, 32.1 ms at p99 and 204.5 ms at worst: the writer's batch and the twin apply. Its 106 ops over 1 s came in 1 spike (minute 43, worst 3783 ms), and every one of them was over 1 s at the null sink too. Instance C, a different us-east host, had the same spike at the same minute, so it came from upstream of the us-east Jetstreams, not from marco or one instance.

Instance B (us-west) is the odd one: p50 218 ms, p99 49.9 s, and 27,153 ops over 1 s, including a backlog of almost a minute from minute 29 to 33 that the us-east instances didn't have. Instances differ a lot, so a deployment should follow the nearest one and treat lag over a few seconds as the source's until a null sink says otherwise.

Per-op matching rebuilds each op's event time as its receive time less its lag, so it pairs ops within 1 ms; 90% paired, and the quantiles don't move between 1 ms and 10 ms of tolerance. The first 5 s of each run are left out: they replay the 1 s overlap a new source resumes with.

CR 1082's 0.48 s stall didn't come back. With the same 1k synthetic ops/s plus live Jetstream, the worst batch during the checkpoint took 134 ms, p99.9 1.9 ms, and the live source's worst lag in the window was 220 ms. The checkpoint took 30 s against 38 s there. CR 1082 saw its stall with the process's RSS at 59 of 61 GiB; here the heap peaked at 0.2 GiB and nothing waited longer than the figure above. That's one checkpoint each, so the stall may be rare rather than gone.

Reads stay up but relationships slow down: p99 102.8 µs in the median second of the checkpoint against 39.0 µs outside it, while has barely moves (4.4 µs). That's the rewrite streaming 38 GB through the page cache and L3 while relationships touch ~100 lists at random; it ends when the checkpoint does. Checkpoints come at 3M ops or daily, so this is about 30 s a day at today's rate.

The process's resident set reads about 71 GiB during a checkpoint, more than the box's 61 GiB: the old and new shard files are both mapped and a page shared by two mappings counts twice. The memory that matters is the heap (under 0.3 GiB) and the page cache, which held both snapshots' hot pages without the box running short: MemAvailable never fell below 58 GiB.

What's left for P2

  • The P2 gate itself. A stranger's path from the README alone: an image, marco serve --jetstream with a snapshot or an empty start, and the queries an appview runs. P1 built the parts; nothing packages them.
  • Decide the has target. Either a boolean membership call on ids (121 ns on a miss today) for appviews that keep marco's ids, or a target for what has returns now (two DIDs in, a URI out, 1.4 µs).
  • Fix the kill test's forced checkpoints (task 949). Admin.Checkpoint during an automatic one should count as the moment to kill, not end the run.
  • Readiness against a replayed relay. The fake relay keeps its frames' recorded times, so the firehose kill test can't time restart to ready. Restamping commit times in the fake, as the Jetstream fake does, would.
  • Checkpoint cost on reads. Relationships p99 roughly triples for the ~30 s of a checkpoint. CR 1082's follow-ups still stand: copy unchanged byte runs with copy_file_range, and size the box for twice the snapshot in page cache.
  • Source lag next to marco's. Public Jetstream instances differed by minutes in this run (us-west fell 53 s behind for five minutes). marco reports its lag per source already; an operator also needs the source's own, or a second source to fail over to.

Method

  • Box. The P0 load box: 32 threads (16 cores), 61 GiB of RAM, one NVMe drive, Linux 7.0, idle apart from the run. One step at a time, except the captures, which recorded beside the lag run and the first kill test.
  • Build. Release binaries from next at CR 1080 (2df62b4f362d), plus two measurement-only additions in this CR: marco-live-bench --source (the App follows a Jetstream for the whole bench, and the restart is timed to ready) and marco-harness lag --events-out (each op's lag, to pair two consumers op by op).
  • Snapshot. The whole network in format v3 as CR 1082 built it: 2.97B edges, 38.5 GB, 64 shards.
  • Captures. 60 min of live Jetstream (jetstream2.us-east, follows and blocks, 128,224 messages) and 10 min of the relay's firehose (bsky.network, 385,540 frames).
  • Kill test. marco-harness killtest: marco serve on a copy of the snapshot following a fake source replaying the capture; Jetstream at 300 and 150 messages/s (4 to 9 times live), the firehose at 1,000 frames/s. A watcher outside the harness logs the marco pid and the snapshot directory every 200 ms, which is where the checkpoint timings come from.
  • Targets. marco-live-bench on a copy of the snapshot, one reader thread for 20 s per phase, the App following live Jetstream, the 60 min capture at 10 times its rate from a fake, or live Jetstream plus 1,000 synthetic ops/s. The restart reopens after the bench's writes (3M ops plus the ceiling's, all in segments) and waits for the source to be under 1 s behind. Delete by URI runs on the engine after 3M firehose-shaped ops.
  • Lag. marco-harness lag, four consumers at once for 60 min: marco (an in-process App on a copy of the snapshot) and three null sinks, on jetstream2.us-east (A), jetstream1.us-west (B) and jetstream1.us-east (C).
  • Reproduce. internal/run-p1-gate.sh runs each step; internal/p1_report.py collect turns its output into internal/raw-p1/gate/*.json, and internal/p1_report.py writes this page from those, CR 1082's raw-p1/live-bench-3.json and P0's raw/.
