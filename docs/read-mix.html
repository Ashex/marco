Two weeks of one account's Bluesky app traffic (2026-10-21 to 2026-11-04, web and iOS, 10,372 requests through its PDS), each endpoint traced through the AppView's source to the graph lookups it makes, sized with a few hundred public AppView responses, and turned into marco calls per active user.

  • Nothing de-duplicates across calls in one response: each embed kind hydrates profiles on its own, so a feed page makes 2-7 getRelationships calls with overlapping subjects (hydration/hydrator.ts:838-844, 1016).
  • No batch caps: each subject or pair set goes in one call.
  • List hits aren't de-duplicated: each subject that comes back blocked or muted by a list adds 2 subscription lookups (hydration/hydrator.ts:310-314, hydration/graph.ts:163-174).
  • getTrends and two suggestion endpoints send getBlockExistence(viewer × D) next to getRelationships(viewer, D), which already has those fields.
  • Follow-back, threadgate violations and follower counts are computed when records are indexed, not per read.
  • fillPage re-runs the whole pipeline up to 10× when filtering leaves less than half a page (api/util.ts:39-85); getFeed doesn't.
  • No hydration cache in the AppView; whatever production caches is in its Go dataplane, not in the repo.
  • chat.bsky.* is a separate service; it calls getProfiles and getPosts as the user.

3. Fan-out per response

Distinct actors per response go to one relationships call; block pairs per response. Feeds and threads: 28 public feed pages and 160 public threads (getPostThreadV2 with the client's parameters), resampled by gzip size to the logged responses' sizes (5 nearest per decile). Thread actors include the direct replies the branching factor trims, which the AppView hydrates anyway. Notifications: windows of 40 over the account's own public likes. Trends: 92 distinct actors across 20 trends. Suggested follows: 49 per call. 253 public AppView requests in all, at most 8/s and 4 in flight.

The logged sizes agree with the samples: the client's feed pages are 15.8 to 23.9 kB gzipped (p10 to p90), public pages of 30 are 14.3 to 19.8 kB. Threads are skewed: the median opened thread hydrates 8 actors, p90 29. Of the notification authors, 18% are followed by the viewer.

Share of calls and of server CPU by call type, this mix against perf.html's hand-picked one.

Against the hand-picked mix: relationships is 68% of calls here, 25% there, and at a mean batch of 21 (feeds), 36 (notifications) and 92 (trends), not 50. Single has and filter calls barely appear: the AppView checks blocks inside relationships, and a pair check is a relationships call of 1 to 3. Counts and known followers come only with profiles. In pages (followers lists) don't appear at all in this client's traffic. Per call the mix costs 23.2 µs of server CPU against 31.5 µs for the hand-picked one.

Run it: marco-harness perf-inputs --snapshot SNAP --out inputs.json (draws the active, heavy and popular pools and, on a snapshot with lists, list URIs), then marco-harness perf --inputs inputs.json --mix internal/read-mix.json …. A shape whose pool is missing (list shapes on a snapshot without lists) is left out and named in the output's meta. The hand-picked mix runs as before with an inline --mix.

5. What one active user costs

Server CPU per active user-second by shape: calls per second × CPU per call.

One active user makes 0.20 app requests a second (0.12 to endpoints with graph lookups), which become 0.45 marco calls and 3.8 actor lookups a second, about 11 µs of server CPU. perf.html's server holds the hand-picked mix at 474k calls/s, 14.9 threads busy at 31.5 µs a call. At this mix's 23.2 µs a call those threads serve about 643k calls/s: 1.42M concurrently active users on one 16-thread server (the hand-picked mix's call rate would have said 1.05M). Half that, for headroom, is 0.71M.

That's a CPU estimate from per-shape costs, not a run. Batch sizes here run from 1 to 150 where perf.html measured 50, so the per-subject slope (1.12 µs per subject for follow and block both ways, 0.56 for block only, on a 13.2 µs call) carries the estimate. Every response is also exactly one AppView pass: fillPage refills and the AppView's repeated calls for embeds would add to it.

6. What marco can't answer

LookupWhy
Lists blocking the viewer (blockedByList)Needs each subject's block-list subscriptions (listblock records), out of scope by decision. marco gives the other half: the lists the viewer is on (ListIn listitem).
List blocks between third partiesgetBlockExistence also checks both sides' block lists: same listblock dependency.
Mutes, mute lists, thread mutesPrivate (bsync), not records. marco can check a page against the viewer's mute lists once the AppView knows them (list_mutes).
A batch of unrelated pairsgetBlockExistence takes many (a, b) pairs; marco's relationships takes one actor and many others. The mix sends one call per first actor, 0.18 calls/s per user at a mean batch of 2.4: 40% of calls for 25% of the CPU, nearly all call overhead.
Known followers' totalThe AppView shows 5 names and a count of the whole intersection. Intersect pages ids (limit 10,000) and returns both sides' lengths, not the intersection's.
Known likersLikes name posts, not actors.
Feeds and timelinesSkeletons come from feed generators and the timeline store; marco only supplies the follow set (follows_all).

Method and assumptions

  • Logs: the PDS's Caddy access log, proxied app.bsky.* and chat.bsky.* requests, CORS preflights and the PDS's own getPreferences left out. One account on two clients: a small sample of real use, not a population. Only aggregates are kept (internal/raw-mix/logs.json).
  • Active time: requests split into sessions at 120 s gaps (the 30 s notification poll keeps an open app in one session); each session counts from its first request to its last plus 30 s. 60 s gaps give 11.86 h, 120 s 12.46 h.
  • Public AppView samples: unauthenticated, so no viewer state; they size responses, not relationships. The personalised For You feed (83% of the client's pages) can't be fetched logged out; public feeds of 30 stand in, resampled to its sizes.
  • Assumed, not measured: the share of a page's actors the viewer follows (feeds 0.3, threads 0.15, profiles 0.5; notifications 0.18 is measured); known followers per profile (0: 20%, 2: 20%, 5: 60%); 30% of users with a mute or block list subscription, 1.7 lists each; block pairs grouped 1.4 to a first actor; getSuggestedFollowsByActor with social proof on.
  • CPU per call: perf.html's closed-loop server CPU at 16 threads (internal/raw-perf/closed-h2-16.json): a has for the call itself, relationships 50×2 for the per-subject slope, filter 50 for list rules, out page 50 for pages, followed-by-you-follow for known followers.
  • Actor pickers in the mix file: viewers from active (actors with 50 to 5,000 follows, each with up to 100 of its follows for the followed share), subjects from popular (the subject of a random follow), lists drawn about as often as they have members.
